# Research protocol: viral is a hypothesis, not just a view count

## Capture objectively
- URL and creator credit
- Published date (only if known)
- Observation timestamp (when metrics were observed)
- Views, likes, comments, shares — leave unknowns blank
- Length in seconds (if known)
- Hook text (quote briefly, with context)
- Visual sequence at 0s, 1s, 3s, 5s, reveal, end
- Sound/audio, if you actually listened
- Cut rhythm, reframing, transition, text treatment, payoff
- Authentic footage vs. official trailer vs. fan concept vs. AI-generated or uncertain
- Permission status if footage might be reused

## Analyze carefully
1. Engagement rate is descriptive; it is not a causal proof of why a video spread.
2. Prefer comparisons within the same category and similar posting ages.
3. Do not label a post 'viral' unless you document criteria and evidence.
4. A strong result on one clip is a *candidate pattern*; 5+ similar examples and your own tests are stronger evidence.
5. Check if edits misrepresent the game or reuse material without authorization.

## Suggested thresholds (testable, not universal facts)
- Candidate study clip: 10k+ views or unusually high share rate for its creator.
- High-priority study clip: unusually high share/engagement ratio relative to similar videos.
- Benchmark your own posts against your own historical baseline.
